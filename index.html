Why the Moon’s south pole is hard

A lunar “day” lasts about a month: roughly two weeks of sunshine, then two weeks of night. And at the south pole the Sun never climbs overhead — it stays as low as a winter sunset on Earth, slowly circling the horizon.

Because the Sun sits so low, any ridge or crater rim a few kilometers away can block it for days. Earth — which a lander must see to call home — can hide behind the same skyline. Move the landing spot a little, or shift the date by a week, and a great site can turn into a dead one. That is why NASA mission planners agonize over where and when.

Think of it like a remote cabin where the solar power and the phone line each cut out for days at a time, on their own schedules. Mission planning is picking the spot and the week where both stay on — this tool lets you try it yourself.

Try CR1 vs SR1 on the same week, then slip the start date — small moves can flip which site survives. The verdict grades your whole mission window; the time slider explores moments inside it.

Communications need Earth above the terrain plus a minimum-elevation margin; power needs the Sun clear of ridges, then a rough panel estimate (area × efficiency × tilt) compared to a battery floor. The critical window is Sun + Earth together — hours you can recharge while talking to Earth with no relay. Use the ? controls on the metrics for how each number is calculated.

Provenance & limits

  • Horizons: bundled JPL observer az/el for CR1/SR1 (Jan 2027); live API or analytic fallback otherwise — source labeled on scrub readout.
  • Horizons (terrain): LOLA 5 m near-field + 320 m overview for CR1/SR1 only; gaps never invented.
  • Regional “Find best location” uses a ~10 km polar-cap grid with LOLA 320 m overview horizons (2° bins) — coarse ranking, not a meter-perfect global optimum.
  • Custom / curated / Apollo / CLPS catalog entries use an optimistic sphere horizon — labeled explicitly; do not treat as LOLA-grade.
  • Rank / date / search scores use the same assessMission engine as the badges; non-study catalog sites remain sphere-optimistic.
  • 3D markers: coral ■ chosen (Site A), amber ◆ search hits, teal ● catalog, violet ◎ Site B. Default terrain layer is mesh/wireframe.
  • 3D display lighting ≠ authoritative observer Sun/Earth status. Quest VR runtime is not shipped — export JSON for external viz.
