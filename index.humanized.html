Why landing near the Moon’s south pole is tricky

A lunar day lasts about a month: roughly two weeks of daylight followed by two weeks of darkness. Near the south pole, the Sun stays low above the horizon, much like a winter sunset on Earth.

Nearby ridges and crater rims can block the Sun for days. They can also block the view of Earth that a lander needs to communicate. A small change in landing spot or date can make a big difference.

Imagine a remote cabin where both the solar power and phone connection cut out for days, but on different schedules. The challenge is finding a place and time when both are available.

Compare CR1 and SR1 during the same week, then try changing the start date. The grade covers the whole mission window; the slider lets you explore individual moments within it.

The lander needs a clear view of the Sun to generate power and a clear view of Earth to communicate directly. The most useful times are when both are visible, so the lander can recharge while staying in touch without a relay.

About the data and its limits

  • Sun and Earth positions use bundled JPL data for CR1 and SR1 in January 2027. Other dates may use a live source or an estimate; the time readout identifies the source.
  • Detailed terrain horizons are available for CR1 and SR1. The planner uses 5 m local terrain and a 320 m regional overview; it does not fill in missing data.
  • The regional location search checks an approximate 10 km grid using 320 m terrain data. It helps narrow down options but does not find a perfect global optimum.
  • Custom and many catalog sites do not have detailed terrain horizons in this tool. Their results assume a smooth lunar surface and may be more optimistic than reality.
  • Site rankings, date suggestions, and regional searches use the same assessment rules as the mission grades. Results for sites without detailed terrain remain estimates.
  • Map markers show your selected site A (coral ■), search results (amber ◆), known sites (teal ●), and comparison site B (violet ◎). The default map shows a terrain wireframe.
  • Map lighting is for illustration; it does not determine the Sun/Earth status or mission grade. A VR viewer is not included, but you can export the data as JSON for use elsewhere.
